Property Tax Appeal Automation

Commercial Property Services Firm

Build time: 10 days | Current volume: 30+ appeals/year | Status: running

The Problem

A commercial property management and consulting firm helped clients appeal their property tax assessments. The process involved:

  • Client intake (contact info, property details, assessment amount)
  • Property research (comparable sales, assessment methodology, jurisdiction rules)
  • Valuation analysis (calculate fair market value)
  • Document preparation (appeal filing requirements vary by jurisdiction)
  • Filing and deadline tracking (each county has different deadlines and forms)
  • Client communication and status updates
  • Hearing prep and evidence compilation

Each appeal took 15–20 hours of manual work spread over weeks. The firm was managing 30+ appeals annually. Most of the work was document-gathering and tracking — not analysis.

The Challenge

Property tax appeals are jurisdiction-specific. There's no standard form, no single deadline, and no one right way to do it. The firm had learned through experience which steps mattered in Illinois, Indiana, and Michigan. But that knowledge was scattered across documents, emails, and people's heads.

And every appeal had edge cases. One property had been recently renovated; another was part of a complex. These affected valuation and filing strategy.

The Solution

We built an end-to-end automation system that:

  • Accepts client intake data via form or email
  • Extracts property details and assessment information
  • Identifies the correct jurisdiction and deadlines
  • Automatically pulls public records (comparable sales, prior assessments, property history)
  • Generates a preliminary valuation analysis
  • Creates the jurisdiction-specific filing documents (with required formats and signatures)
  • Tracks deadlines and sends reminders to staff and clients
  • Compiles evidence packets and hearing prep documents
  • Maintains a master database of all appeals and outcomes for future reference

The architecture: Instead of trying to build one-size-fits-all workflow, we built it in layers. Core logic (intake, research, analysis) is shared. Jurisdiction-specific layers (filing requirements, deadlines, forms) branch out. Staff can override or add edge-case logic when needed, but 85% of appeals flow through automatically.

How We Built It Fast

Speed Through Constraint

We could have spent 6 weeks perfecting the system. Instead, we focused on the 80/20: what are the most common steps? Which 20% of edge cases account for 80% of complexity?

We scoped the initial build to handle straightforward residential and commercial properties in three jurisdictions. Special cases (environmental remediation, recent major construction) could be handled manually with a streamlined review process rather than full automation.

The Build

Days 1–2: Diagnosed the workflow. Mapped intake → research → filing → tracking. Identified three key decision points where staff needed to make judgment calls.

Days 3–6: Built core intake and research modules. Integrated with county property records APIs and comparable sales databases.

Days 7–9: Built filing document generation and deadline tracking.

Day 10: Tested against 10 prior appeals. Refined. Deployed.

Post-Launch Learning

Week 2–4: Staff used the system on real appeals. We monitored for edge cases and patterns. We found 3 scenarios we hadn't anticipated. We added handling for those. Now the system handles 85% without human intervention.

The Business Impact

Capacity: The firm went from processing 30 appeals/year (at 15–20 hours each) to being able to handle 80+ appeals/year with the same staff. They're now expanding to new jurisdictions without adding headcount.

Quality: No more missed deadlines. No more incomplete filings. The system enforces process consistency.

Client experience: Clients get automated status updates. They know when their appeal was filed, when the hearing is scheduled, and what to expect.

Institutional knowledge: The system now contains 3+ years of appeal history, outcomes by jurisdiction, and what works. New staff don't have to learn from scratch.
